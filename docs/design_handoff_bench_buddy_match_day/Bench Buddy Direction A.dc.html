13b · Two kinds of no
The consequence is stated before the choice: Jack stays on. Hugo stays on the bench. A cancelled sub always means someone else keeps playing, and that is the part a coach forgets.
A cancelled sub is always temporary. Hugo stays on the bench and moves to the front of the queue, so he is first on at the next interval and the app still owes him the minutes.
Taking a player out for the rest of the game is not here — that is the injured-player flow, which closes their minutes properly. One question, one answer.
Keep the sub is the primary button. The dialog opened from a single tap and should cost a single tap to leave.

Why this order
The three numbers come first because they are what the coach came to change, and every other line on the screen is derived from them.
Under them, the interval chips answer the question the numbers raise. Only the intervals that divide evenly for today's turnout are offered, and the best fit is filled green — the coach taps a number rather than reading a sentence about fairness.
Then four one-line rows carrying their current value — Random, Every 4′, Every third, 12 players. They read as already answered, so a coach in a hurry goes straight to Build new rotation. Squad lives here too, behind Manage squad, because the turnout is set on Who's here? before this screen.
Tapping a row expands it in place (4b) and turns it dark, so what you overrode this week stays obvious when you scroll back.
No standing red warning. Rebuilding only costs something when a game is already running, so the caution moves into 4c and appears only then.
